What this is

A Year 12 IB CAS group project at Dhirubhai Ambani International School, Mumbai, academic year 2026–27. It has two connected parts: technology-literacy sessions through September and October, and an AI-enabled, social-impact hackathon that closes the project in late October.

Rules, including AI and vibe coding

Draft rules. The participant rulebook, published before the event, is final.

  • You may use AI tools freely: code assistants, chatbots and generative tools are all allowed.
  • Vibe coding is welcome. Generating most of your code with AI is fine.
  • Submit three things: a working MVP, a pitch deck, and an explanation of how the solution would be implemented.
  • Teams build during the build period, with organiser support on Discord.
  • Your solution must address a social problem linked to the UN Sustainable Development Goals.
  • Follow the participant rulebook, published before the event.

How the event runs

From opening to results. Exact timings will be in the rulebook.

  1. Opening ceremony

    Welcome, introductions and how the event will run.

  2. Guest speaker

    A talk from an invited guest speaker (to be announced).

  3. Challenge briefing

    The theme is revealed. Juniors receive structured problem statements; seniors get the broader SDG theme.

  4. Q&A

    Ask the organisers anything about the theme, rules or submissions before the build starts.

  5. Build period

    Design, prototype and prepare your pitch. Organisers hold office hours on Discord in rotating shifts.

  6. Submission

    Upload your MVP, pitch deck and implementation explanation before the deadline.

  7. Judging and pitches

    Teams pitch to the judges. Results and prizes follow.

What judges look for

  • Solution quality and feasibility

    Does it address a real social problem, and could it realistically work?

  • Implementation thinking

    How clearly the team explains who the solution serves, how it would be rolled out and what it needs.

  • Prototype (MVP)

    A working minimum viable product that demonstrates the core idea.

  • Presentation and pitch

    A clear, honest pitch that communicates the problem, the solution and its impact.

Exact rubric, weighting and judges to be confirmed.

How sessions run

  • Adapted to the audience

    Content is matched to what people already know. Where needed, a session starts with basic coding and technology concepts before moving to anything more advanced.

  • Offline, at community centres

    Sessions take place at NGO and community centres, possibly with organisations that run several locations. Each one goes ahead with school/CAS approval and the organisation’s approval.

    Preparation sessions for hackathon participants run online before the event, covering the AI, coding and pitching skills teams will use.

Different team members lead different sessions, but the whole organising team takes part in running the final hackathon.

Why we’re running this

Why a hackathon

We started from the school's previous coding challenge: algorithmic problems of increasing difficulty, solved in languages like Python or C++. AI tools can now solve many of those problems, so a no-AI rule would be almost impossible to enforce. Pure competitive programming also attracts a fairly narrow group of students.

A build-focused hackathon is open to more students and connects directly to real social problems. So AI tools are allowed, and judges look at the whole solution: the idea, the prototype, the implementation plan and the pitch.

The project responds to unequal access to practical technology and digital skills, and to the need for new ideas on social problems linked to the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

The two IB CAS strands this project covers

Service The literacy programme serves NGO and community groups and hackathon participants by building practical technology knowledge, while the hackathon focuses on solutions to social needs and the SDGs.

Creativity Teams identify problems, design original technology-based solutions, build a minimum viable product, and communicate their implementation through a pitch and deck.

Questions

Short answers for participants, parents and teachers. Answers marked "to be confirmed" will be updated here as details are finalised.

How do I register?

Registration opens in October together with the participant rulebook. The registration link will appear on this page.

Who can take part?To be confirmed

School students, in two divisions. Junior is Grade 10 and below; Senior is Grade 11 and above. The exact lower grade for Junior (Grade 6 or Grade 8) and whether college students can enter Senior are still to be confirmed.

Is there an age limit?To be confirmed

Discord requires users to be at least 13. The junior division's lower grade limit, and arrangements for younger participants, will be confirmed before registration opens.

When is it, and how long is the build?To be confirmed

It is planned for 24–25 October 2026, Saturday to Sunday. The build is proposed to last 24 hours.

What does it cost?To be confirmed

The working figure is ₹2,000 per team. The current plan is to use part of the registration proceeds for prizes and donate the rest to NGOs and community organisations connected with the literacy programme. The final model is still being decided.

How big can a team be?

Up to 4 people per team. Whether you can enter on your own is still to be confirmed.

Can we use AI tools?

Yes. AI tools are allowed, vibe coding included. Teams are judged on the overall solution and its feasibility, their implementation thinking, the MVP and the pitch, not on how sophisticated the code is.

What's the theme?

Social problems linked to the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Juniors receive structured problem statements; seniors get a broader theme and define their own problem.

What do we submit?

Three things: a working MVP, a pitch deck, and an explanation of how the solution would actually be implemented.

What are the prizes?To be confirmed

Cash prizes and non-cash prizes such as subscriptions are planned. The amounts and awards will be announced closer to the event.

Is it online?

Yes. The plan is to run it online on Discord, for announcements, team channels, voice and questions. Organisers stay available in rotating office-hours shifts during the build.

Do I need to attend the sessions to compete?To be confirmed

We'll confirm this when registration opens. The online preparation sessions are for hackathon participants and will help you get ready.
